The deck's method, run on this page's data

The deck age-aligns every store, averages the stores trading at each month of age, charges the format working-capital curve and reads the first crossing of the format's average capital. That is done here on actuals to Aug 26. The bold line is that average; the thin lines are the stores behind it; the small figures under the axis are how many stores are in the average at that age. Where the average stops, the data stops — the deck's line carries on past that point, and the dashed path shows what it needs from the stores to reach the deck's crossing.

What the company's balance sheet says

Days of cost of goods sold, month-end, whole company · MIS balance sheet console, Apr 24 – Aug 26. This card does not respond to the store filters: the balance sheet is one number for the network.

Net working-capital days = days of stock − days of creditors, the deck's own definition (debtors are nil: it is a cash business). The dashed line is the same measure as the 21 Aug business update reports it (Sep 25 → Aug 26); it sits 6–8 days above the console line because the update's creditor base is narrower — its own "82% of inventory funded by suppliers" implies about 22 creditor days against the 30 on the console's Sundry Creditors. Month-end creditors are lumpy, so read the trend, not one point.

What the payback maths assumes

Dailies run from 28 days at M1 to −8 days at M36 — below zero the store is financed by its vendors and working capital adds to recovery. The daily curve is actual to M16 and a straight line after that.

How working capital enters payback, and what the balance sheet changes

Cash blocked in WC(m) = NWC days(age m) × COGS(m) ÷ 30 Recovery(m) = Σ Contribution(1…m) − Cash blocked in WC(m)

Three things the company series says that the format curve cannot

Stock days have not fallen with age. The curve has a daily's stock-driven NWC dropping from 28 days to about 4 by its first birthday. The company's inventory days have sat between 27 and 37 days of COGS for two years while the fleet aged from a median of 5 months to 12. Whatever is bringing net working capital down, it is not stock turning faster.

Vendor credit is doing all the work. Creditor days have gone from 14 in Apr 24 to 25–30 through 2026. Net days went from +13 to −2 over the same span almost entirely on that line. This is real cash — but it is negotiated centrally, it is not a property of any store's age, and it can be withdrawn by a vendor in a way that a stock reduction cannot.

In 2026 the curve and the company roughly agree; in 2025 they did not. Weighted across the 34 stores at their ages, the format curve implies 4–7 days through Apr–Aug 26 — close to the 3–6 days the business update reports and a few days above the console's −2 to +5. Through 2025 the network was holding 10–15 days more than the curve assumed (₹150–300L). So the curve happens to land near the network's current position, but it got there by a different route — it assumes stock days fall with store age, and what actually happened is creditor days rose.

What this does not settle

The balance sheet is one number for the network: it includes distribution-centre stock and the institutional business, and it cannot say whether Berhampore carries more days than Hatiara. The store-level file (Store_wise_NWC_Till_Jul_26.xlsx) is still the thing that answers which stores are sitting on stock and which are being carried by their vendors. Until it lands, the Working capital → Excluded toggle shows recovery on store contribution alone, and this tab shows how far the format curve is from the company's own books.

Other current assets stand at ₹2,209L and other current liabilities at ₹629L (Aug 26). Neither is in the deck's definition of working capital; the assets line is where lease deposits and GST credit sit, and it is larger than the whole stock line.

Exact method

Every number on this page is produced by the steps below. Nothing is smoothed, indexed or back-solved.

1. Source data

Store P&L: Store Wise Summary_August 26.xlsx, one tab per store, monthly Feb-23 → Aug-26. Four lines are used per store-month: Total Net Sales, Rs TCI, Rs Store Opex, Rs Store Contribution. All figures Rs Lacs.
Capital: SumoSave_23Store_Capex_Preop.xlsx — "Capex - Total Cost" (gross of GST) as at 31-Mar-26, plus pre-operating expense. For the 7 stores outside that file, capex is taken from the Fixed Assets_SRVPL register (gross, to Dec-25). For the 4 stores in neither, the format average is used. Every store is tagged with its capital source in the league table.
Security deposit: SumoSave Storewise Datasets.xlsx, Storewise Capex & SD tab.

2. Store age, not calendar time

Every store is re-based to its own opening date. Month of operation M1 = the first full calendar month after the launch month. The launch month itself is a partial month and is dropped, which is the same convention the deck uses — this was verified by reproducing the deck's supermarket curve exactly (M1: Net Sales 59.631, TCI 8.680, Opex 5.952, Contribution 2.728).

3. Monthly contribution

Store Contribution(m) = Rs TCI(m) − Rs Store Opex(m) COGS(m) = Total Net Sales(m) − Rs TCI(m)

Store Opex is store-level only: rent, salaries, electricity and store overheads. It sits above SCM cost (~2.1% of sales), marketing (~0.9%) and all corporate overhead — none of which is charged against capital anywhere in this analysis, exactly as in the deck.

4. Working capital

Cash blocked in WC(m) = NWC Days(age m) × COGS(m) / 30

NWC Days is the format-level curve by month of operation published in the deck (supermarkets M1–M36, dailies M1–M16 actual, then straight-lined to −8.0 days at M36 per the deck's own stated endpoint). Store-level NWC days are not available in any dataset supplied — until Store_wise_NWC_Till_Jul_26.xlsx arrives, every store of a format carries the same days curve. Switch Working capital → Excluded to remove this assumption entirely and see purely store-actual numbers.

5. Capital recovered

Cumulative recovery(m) = Σ Contribution(1…m) − Cash blocked in WC(m) % recovered(m) = Cumulative recovery(m) ÷ Capital employed

Note the working-capital term is a stock at month m, not a cumulative flow — only the current month's blocked cash is netted off. This is the deck's own treatment and it makes the line jumpy: a single month's inventory build moves it by Rs 20–40L on a Rs 86L base.

6. Capital employed

Three bases, switchable at the top of the page:

Capex (gross of GST, as at 31-Mar-26) Capex + Pre-op ← the deck's headline basis Capex + Pre-op + Security deposit ← full cash committed per store

7. Payback

Paid back = the first month from which % recovered stays at or above 100% for every subsequent observed month, interpolated within that month

This is the durable crossing, not the first touch. The deck reports the first touch, which on the supermarket line happens at M28.5 but falls back below at M30 and only holds from M31. Where a store has not yet crossed, Projected extends the store's last three months of monthly contribution forward at a flat rate, with no growth assumed:

Projected payback = current age + (Capital − recovery to date) ÷ mean monthly Store Contribution over the last 3 months

Capped at 72 months; beyond that the store is shown as "not in 72m". This is deliberately conservative — it credits the store with no further sales ramp, no margin improvement and no working-capital release.

8. Durable contribution breakeven

Breakeven M = the first month of operation from which monthly Store Contribution stays positive for every subsequent observed month

Observable at 5–15 months rather than 20–30, so it exists for almost every store. It is the earliest honest read on whether a store is working.

9. What is a judgement call, and whose

  • Cluster (Metro / Upcountry) is Claude's classification from store names — no location field exists in any supplied dataset. Confirm with management before quoting it.
  • Status. Kanchrapara is flagged as relocated to Kalyani; Chinsura and Kestopur as exiting wef Jan-27, per the deck. All three are included by default and can be filtered out.
  • Format. Converted Dailies and Compact Supermarkets are shown as their own group rather than folded into "Daily". The deck folds them in for contribution but excludes them from capex entirely — the two populations do not match.
  • Pre-op is incomplete for Maheshtala (Rs 2.51L) and Sodepur (Rs 0.03L) because the pre-op source starts Apr-24 and both opened earlier. Metiabruz carries Rs 17.01L of "Store Launch Expenses" against Rs 0.79–2.19L everywhere else. Those three stores' capital is understated, understated and overstated respectively.
  • GST is inside capex. If input tax credit is claimable on furniture, electrical and plant (it generally is; Sec 17(5) blocks only immovable-property works contract, ~4% of the register), the capital base is overstated by up to ~15% and every payback here is correspondingly long.

10. Where this departs from the deck, and by how much

The deck reports 28.9 months for a supermarket and 28.6 for a daily on capex + pre-op. Applying the deck's own method to this page's data — age-align every store, average the stores trading at each month of age, charge the format working-capital curve, read the first crossing — gives 28.8 months for supermarkets. The deck's number is reproducible; the disagreement is not about arithmetic.

Supermarkets: the average is not a store. The mean curve is five stores at M1, four at M9, three at M12–M24, two at M27 and Active Acres alone from M30. Its crossing at M28.8 is Active Acres and Maheshtala blended, and nothing else — Active Acres actually crossed at M30.6 on its own capital (₹104L, not the ₹86L average), Maheshtala at M23.8. The other three supermarkets are at −56% (Serampore, M25), +11% (Krishnanagar, M12) and −17% (Metiabruz, M7) recovered. Two of five have paid back; the deck's line describes those two.

Dailies: the deck's curve runs past the data. On this page's actuals the daily mean curve reaches −13% at M12 (n=7), −8% at M15 (n=4), and from M18 onward is Sodepur alone. It never crosses. The deck's daily curve is actual to about M16–M19 and extrapolated to M36, with sales still ramping and NWC days straight-lined to −8 — so working capital becomes a source of cash in years two and three. Getting from −13% at M12 to 100% at M28.6 needs ₹2.8L a month per store of contribution plus working-capital release. The seven dailies that have reached M12 produced ₹0.89L a month between them in Aug 26, and the fleet of 22 has generated −₹25L of cumulative contribution in total. That is the whole gap: not a different formula, but a curve that assumed a ramp the stores have not delivered.

The seven converted and compact stores are outside the deck entirely. They are excluded from its capex average and folded into the daily contribution line. Here they carry their own capital — ₹368L, a fifth of the network's — against −₹191L of cumulative contribution. Including them is why the network as a whole reads −8% recovered while the deck implies a 2½-year business.

The deck's projected months came in below projection. Its "existing store" base carried Aug, Sep and Oct 26 as forecasts. August is now actual and the supermarket line diverges from the deck from M7 for that reason alone.

The projection here does not ramp. Where a store has not crossed, this page extends its last three months of contribution flat. The deck's forward cases apply +15% and +26.5% to sales and 7.5–10 bps a month to margin. Neither is wrong as a scenario; they answer different questions. This page answers "on what these stores are doing now, when do they pay back" — and for 28 of 34 the answer is not inside six years.
