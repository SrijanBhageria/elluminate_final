SumoSave | The neighbourhood grocery store playbook: standardised formats, repeat purchasing and maturity-led economics

Twenty-nine standard-format stores provide evidence of a common grocery proposition: relatively consistent baskets and margins, a rising transaction base as stores mature, and improving store contribution as sales cross operating-cost thresholds. Store throughput still differs by catchment, making location selection central to scaling returns.

01 · THE STORE PLAYBOOK

A standardised operating model across two neighbourhood store formats

SumoSave operates two standardised neighbourhood grocery formats: Dailies and Supermarkets. Each follows a recognisable merchandise and cost structure. As stores mature, higher transaction density and a growing share of sales from returning customers support increased sales productivity and contribution.

Replication in neighbourhood grocery is not about every store generating identical sales. The evidence shows consistent operating characteristics across formats, while local catchment demand and store-level throughput determine the pace of sales ramp and profitability.

07 · CUSTOMER LOYALTY

A recurring neighbourhood customer base increasingly supports store sales

SumoSave's neighbourhood proposition is designed around recurring local shopping missions. In the observed cohort data, core customers account for 82% of total recorded customer sales in Dailies and 79% in Supermarkets. Their behaviour illustrates the role of an established customer base in sustaining revenue, while the all-customer cohort view is retained to assess acquisition and subsequent activity without retrospectively selecting only core customers. Month 0 (the month of first purchase, usually partial) is excluded from the post-acquisition trend.
